iPhone screen replacement: LCD vs OLED, which should you choose?

Got two quotes for the same iPhone screen and one is much cheaper? The difference is usually the type of screen. Here's what LCD and OLED replacement screens really mean, and how to pick the right one for your phone and budget.

Which iPhones have OLED or LCD screens?

First, find out what your iPhone came with from the factory:

  • OLED from the factory: every iPhone from the iPhone 12 onward (including all mini, Plus, Pro and Pro Max models), plus the iPhone X, XS, XS Max, 11 Pro and 11 Pro Max.
  • LCD from the factory: iPhone XR, iPhone 11, iPhone SE (2nd and 3rd generation), and iPhone 8 and older.

If your iPhone came with an LCD, the choice is simple: it gets an LCD replacement, and a good-quality one will look like the original. The LCD vs OLED question only matters if your iPhone came with OLED, because then there are cheaper LCD screens that also fit it.

LCD vs OLED: what's the difference?

LCD screens use a light behind the whole screen that shines through the pixels. Even "black" areas are lit a little from behind, so blacks look dark gray.

OLED screens have pixels that light up on their own. A black pixel is simply turned off, so you get true black, stronger contrast and more vivid colors. OLED is also thinner, because it doesn't need the extra light layer.

That's why Apple uses OLED on its current iPhones, and why putting an LCD into a phone that was built for OLED is a noticeable step down.

The 4 types of iPhone replacement screens

For an iPhone that came with OLED, repair shops generally use one of these:

1. Genuine Apple screen

A new screen made by Apple. It's the same quality as your original screen, and it's the most expensive option.

2. Refurbished original screen

An original Apple display panel with new glass on top. The picture quality matches the original, and it usually costs less than a brand-new genuine screen.

3. Aftermarket OLED ("soft" or "hard" OLED)

OLED screens made by other manufacturers. You keep true blacks and vivid colors, at a lower price. Quality varies by supplier. "Soft" (flexible) OLEDs are generally closer to the original than "hard" (rigid) ones.

4. Aftermarket LCD (often called "incell")

The cheapest option. It works and responds to touch, but compared with OLED it's dimmer, has grayer blacks and less vivid colors, can sit slightly thicker in the frame, and often uses more battery. On Pro models, cheaper screens may also not support the smooth 120Hz ProMotion refresh rate.

Which one should you choose?

Choose OLED (genuine, refurbished or quality aftermarket) if:

  • Your iPhone is fairly new, or it's a Pro or Pro Max model
  • You plan to keep the phone for a few more years
  • You use your phone a lot for photos, videos or outdoors
  • You may sell or trade in the phone later (screen quality affects its value)

An LCD can make sense if:

  • The phone is older and you mainly need it to work again
  • It's a kid's phone or a backup phone
  • You're planning to upgrade soon and just need to get by

Tip when comparing quotes: always ask, "Which type of screen is this price for?" A quote that's much lower than the others is almost always an LCD. That's fine if it's what you want, but you should know before the repair, not after.

About the "Unknown Part" message: on newer iPhones, any screen that isn't from Apple may show an "Unknown Part" notice in Settings. It's informational, and the phone keeps working normally.

How to tell which screen you got

Not sure what was put in your phone after a past repair? Try this quick test:

  1. Go into a dark room and turn your brightness up.
  2. Open a completely black image or video.
  3. Look at the screen. On OLED, it's pure black and blends into the room. On LCD, you'll see a gray glow, especially near the edges.

Thicker black borders around the screen than on the original can also be a sign of an LCD replacement.
