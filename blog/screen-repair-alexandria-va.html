Phone screen repair in Alexandria, VA: what to expect

Cracked your iPhone or Samsung screen? Here's how to tell what kind of damage you have, what affects the price, the different types of replacement screens, and how an on-site repair works from start to finish.

Signs your screen needs replacing

A cracked screen is obvious, but some screen damage is hiding under the glass. Your screen needs a replacement if you notice any of these:

  • Cracks or chips in the glass, even small ones near the edges
  • Black spots or "ink" spreading, which means the display panel under the glass is damaged
  • Green, pink or white lines across the screen
  • Touch problems: parts of the screen don't respond, or the phone taps and types on its own ("ghost touch")
  • Flickering or a completely black screen while the phone still rings or vibrates (here's how to check a black screen)

On almost all modern phones, the glass and the display are bonded together. This means even a crack that only seems to be in the glass is usually fixed by replacing the whole screen.

Is it okay to wait?

A small crack can seem harmless, but it usually gets worse. Here's why it's better not to wait too long:

  • Cracks spread. Everyday pressure and temperature changes make them grow, and a crack that reaches the display can cause dead spots or lines.
  • Moisture and dust get in. A cracked screen lets moisture into the phone more easily, which can damage other parts.
  • Glass splinters. Small pieces of glass can cut your fingers or face.

Until you can get it fixed: put a cheap screen protector or a piece of clear packing tape over the cracked area. It keeps glass splinters in place and helps stop the crack from spreading.

What affects the cost of a screen repair

No single price fits every phone. The main factors are:

  • Your exact model. Newer and "Pro" or "Ultra" models have more advanced screens that cost more.
  • OLED vs. LCD. Most current iPhones and Samsung Galaxy phones use OLED screens, which cost more than LCD screens.
  • The quality of the replacement part (see the next section).
  • Samsung frames. On many Galaxy models, the screen comes as one part with the phone's frame, which can raise the price compared with a similar iPhone.
  • Other damage. A bent frame or a damaged back glass from the same drop may need attention too.

Types of replacement screens

Not all replacement screens are the same, and this is where prices from different shops can vary a lot. In general, you'll see:

  • Original (genuine) screens made by the phone's manufacturer. They have the best color, brightness and touch response, and cost the most.
  • High-quality aftermarket OLED screens. These are close to the original in look and feel, at a lower price.
  • Budget LCD screens used on phones that originally had OLED. They're cheaper, but they're often thicker, less bright, show grayer blacks and can use more battery.

Whichever shop you use, ask which type of screen your quote includes. A very cheap quote often means a lower-quality screen. For a full breakdown, see our guide to iPhone LCD vs OLED replacement screens.

iPhone owners: on newer iPhones, a screen that isn't from Apple may show an "Unknown Part" message in Settings. It's informational, and the phone keeps working. Some features, like True Tone, may behave differently depending on the part.

How to get ready for your repair

  1. Back up your phone to iCloud, Google or a computer if you can. Screen repairs normally don't touch your data, but a backup is always smart.
  2. Know your passcode so you can unlock the phone and test it after the repair.
  3. Take off your case and old screen protector.
  4. Pick a spot with a table and good light, like your kitchen, your desk at work, or a table at a coffee shop.

What to check after the repair

Before you call it done, take a minute to check:

  • Touch works everywhere on the screen, including the edges and corners. Try dragging an app icon around the whole screen.
  • Brightness goes all the way up and down, and colors look normal.
  • The front camera, Face ID or fingerprint unlock, and the earpiece speaker (make a quick call) all work.
  • The screen sits flat in the frame, with no gaps or lifting at the edges.
