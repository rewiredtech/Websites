Phone won't charge? 7 things to check before paying for a repair

Before you assume the worst, run through these quick checks. Several of them take less than a minute, and many charging problems turn out to be something simple.

1. Try a different cable and charger

This is the most common cause. Charging cables get bent and pulled every day, and they often fail near the ends, even when they look fine. Borrow a cable and power adapter that you know work with another phone, and try them.

If your phone charges with the borrowed set, you've found the problem, and a new cable costs much less than a repair. Stick with good-quality or brand-name cables. Very cheap ones can charge slowly or stop working quickly.

2. Try a different power source

Plug into a different wall outlet, not a power strip, laptop USB port or car charger. Laptop and car USB ports often give too little power to charge a modern phone properly, so it may look like the phone isn't charging at all.

3. Check the charging port for lint and debris

Pocket lint slowly packs into the bottom of the charging port. Eventually the cable can't go in all the way, so it only charges at a certain angle or not at all. Shine a flashlight into the port. If you see gray fuzz packed at the bottom, that's probably your problem.

Be careful: power the phone off first. Never use metal objects like pins, paper clips or tweezers, because they can scratch or short the contacts inside. If the lint doesn't come out easily, a technician can clean it safely, usually much faster and cheaper than replacing the port.

4. Restart the phone

Sometimes a software glitch stops the phone from recognizing the charger. A restart clears it. If the screen is frozen or black, try a force restart:

  • iPhone 8 and newer: press and release volume up, press and release volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo appears.
  • Samsung Galaxy: hold the side button and volume down together for about 10 seconds until the phone restarts.

Leave it plugged in for 15–30 minutes afterward. A completely drained battery can take a while before the screen shows anything.

5. Look for a moisture warning

If the port is wet, from rain, a sweaty pocket or a drop in water, many phones will block charging to protect themselves. iPhones show a "Liquid Detected" alert, and Samsung phones show "Moisture detected."

Unplug the cable, gently tap the phone with the port facing down, and leave it somewhere dry with good airflow until the warning goes away. Don't use a hair dryer, and skip the bag of rice. Apple specifically recommends against it, because rice dust can end up in the port.

6. Check battery health

A worn-out battery can act like a charging problem: the phone charges very slowly, jumps around in percentage, or dies at 20–30%.

  • iPhone: go to Settings → Battery → Battery Health. If it says "Service" or the maximum capacity is low, it's time for a battery replacement.
  • Samsung: open the Samsung Members app → Support → Phone diagnostics → Battery status.

Stuck at 80%? That's probably not a problem. Newer iPhones and Samsung phones have battery-protection settings that pause charging at 80% to extend battery life. Check your battery charging settings before booking a repair.

7. Try wireless charging (if your phone supports it)

This is a great test. If your phone charges fine on a wireless pad but not with a cable, the battery is probably okay and the problem is the charging port itself. That's a common, fixable repair.

When it's time for a repair

If you've tried all 7 checks and your phone still won't charge, it's probably a hardware problem, most often one of these:

  • A damaged or worn-out charging port (very common on phones that are a few years old)
  • A battery that has reached the end of its life
  • Damage from a drop or liquid

The good news is that charging ports and batteries are some of the most common repairs, and they're usually much cheaper than buying a new phone.
